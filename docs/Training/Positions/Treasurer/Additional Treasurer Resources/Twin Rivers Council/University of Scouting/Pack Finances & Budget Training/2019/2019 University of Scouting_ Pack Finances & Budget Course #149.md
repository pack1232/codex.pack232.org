## Slide 4: WHY ARE PACK FINANCES AND BUDGETING SO IMPORTANT?

- >$5k can easily flow through a Pack’s account in one year
- Be cognizant that the Pack and its assets are owned by the Charter Organization
- Allows the Committee Chair and the Cubmaster to properly manage and operate the Pack
- “A scout is thrifty” does not mean “A scout is stingy”
- Not everyone follows the first tenant of the Scout Law…

## Slide 5: SELECTING AND TRAINING A PACK TREASURER

- Finance education/career is important
- Know them…can you trust them?
- Recruit them…do not necessarily take the first person to volunteer
- The ideal candidate may not be the best candidate on paper
- Ensure that there is a period of time for the current treasurer to train their successor
  - Having a Pack Treasurer “How To” Manual speeds up onboarding and helps recruit successors

## Slide 6: GOALS FOR A PACK TREASURER

- What is the Cubmaster/Den Leaders trying to achieve in the Scout Year?
  - Big trip(s)?
  - Big purchase?
  - Send scouts to summer camp?
  - Help families in need?
  - Train the leaders?
- Cashflow positive
- Rainy Day fund
- Setting realistic revenue projections
- Identifying necessary recurring and nonrecurring expenses

## Slide 7: PACK FUNDS – BEST PRACTICES

- Have a Pack bank account:
  - Checking
  - Online payments
  - Debit card
  - Rainy day fund
  - Special purchase account
- Two unrelated people on the bank account (Committee Chair & Treasurer)
  - Not the Cubmaster
- Annually audit the account by an independent/financially savvy Pack parent
  - You are also recruiting a future Pack Treasurer at the same time
- Two adult sign off on cash deposits
- Do not sign checks reimbursing yourself or your spouse
- Control on expense reimbursements
  - Adults send receipts to Pack Treasurer and Committee Chair to reimburse
  - Acknowledgement is sent by treasurer to adult with a cc to committee chair
  - Do not net

## Slide 8: CHALLENGES YOU MAY FACE

- getting people to pay fees/dues in a reasonable amount of time
- large cash flow variations due to erratic timing of income and expenses (especially popcorn sales and rechartering fees)
- keeping complete and up to date records
- dealing with unforeseen circumstances (e.g. who is responsible for a bounced check fee?, do you reimburse someone who paid for but couldn’t come to an event?, how do you handle a budget mistake?)

## Slide 9: PACK REVENUES

- Dues:
  - Discount for new members?
  - Collection timing versus recharter (by 1/31)?
    - Per scout = $12 for Boys Life, $33 for registration, and $27 for program/insurance
    - Per unit = $40 for unit liability insurance
  - When setting dues keep in mind that other organized activities have high registration fees
- Fundraising:
  - Popcorn and/or nuts: show&sells, door-to-door, and online
  - Other (see unit money earning application)
- Activity Fees (e.g. camping, camporee, trip, etc.):
  - Don’t give away anything for free
  - Enforce collection on last minute drop outs if no replacement can be found for them
- Grants
  - Free money! You get dollar for dollar versus a % of popcorn
- Donations

## Slide 10: COLLECTION OPTIONS

- Cash – 2 adult confirmation
- Checks
- Paypal – see if Pack website allows Paypal features
  - Consider passing on the ~3% usage charge
- Square – helps with popcorn sales and other fundraising
  - For Pack items, consider passing on the 2.75% usage charge

## Slide 11: PACK EXPENSES

- Recharter (by 1/31)
- Badges, belt loops, and pins
- Recurring versus nonrecurring (e.g. software license) expenses
- Setasides
  - Future pinewood derby track purchase
  - Subsidy to send scouts to summer camp
- Seek to minimize Pack Expenses
  - Sales tax exemption (ST-119.1)
  - Minimize gear purchases (borrow from a Scouts BSA troop)
  - Reuse uniforms, neckerchiefs, caps, belts, and leader materials
