## Slide 6: Treasurer Selection & Training

- Finance education/career is important
- Know them…can you trust them?
- Recruit them…do not necessarily take the first person to volunteer
- Ensure that there is a period of time for the current treasurer to train their successor
  - Having a Pack Treasurer “How To” Manual speeds up onboarding and helps recruit successors

## Slide 8: PACK FUNDS – BEST PRACTICES

- Have a Pack bank account:
  - Checking
  - Online payments
  - Debit card
  - Rainy day fund
  - Special purchase account
- Two unrelated people on the bank account (Committee Chair & Treasurer)
  - Not the Cubmaster
- Annually audit the account by an independent/financially savvy Pack parent
  - You are also recruiting a future Pack Treasurer at the same time
- Two adults sign off on cash deposits
- Do not sign checks reimbursing yourself or your spouse
- Control on expense reimbursements
  - Adults send receipts to Pack Treasurer and Committee Chair to reimburse
  - Acknowledgement is sent by Pack Treasurer to adult with a cc to Committee Chair
  - Do not net – for example, a family pays for full cost for event and then receives reimbursement for any Pack subsidy rather than the net of the two.

## Slide 9: Challenges You May Face

- Getting people to pay fees/dues in a reasonable amount of time
- large cash flow variations due to erratic timing of income and expenses (especially popcorn sales and rechartering fees)
- keeping complete and up to date records
  - keep records to help with future budgeting and transitioning to future Pack Treasurer(s).
  - keep records and receipts digitally for easy access by all, including transparency for current members and reference for future.
- dealing with unforeseen circumstances (e.g., who is responsible for a bounced check fee?, do you reimburse someone who paid for but couldn’t come to an event?, how do you handle a budget mistake?)
- Ownership of Square, Paypal, other online payment system
- Challenge you won’t face – you don’t have to file tax returns for the Pack

## Slide 10: PACK REVENUES

- Dues:
  - Discount for new members to offset $25 one-time joining fee?
  - Collection timing versus recharter (by 12/31)?
    - Per scout = $15 for Scout Life magazine annual subscription, $78 for registration, and $30 for TRC program/insurance
    - Per unit = $100 for unit liability insurance
  - When setting dues, keep in mind that other organized activities have high registration fees
- Fundraising:
  - Popcorn and/or Camp Cards: show&sells, door-to-door, and online
    - Consider accepting a donation (i.e. extra dues) in lieu of a family selling popcorn or participating in fundraisers
  - Other (see unit money earning application)
- Activity Fees (e.g. camping, camporee, trip, etc.):
  - Don’t give away anything for free
  - Enforce collection on last minute drop outs if no replacement can be found for them
- Grants (e.g. Stewarts Holiday Match)
  - Free money! You get dollar for dollar versus a % of popcorn or camp cards
- Donations
  - Note that you cannot ask for donations during fundraiser show-and-sells but you can accept them

## Slide 17: Quickbooks

- Software can make financial work easier, but there is a learning curve
- ~$75/year for quickbooks online
- Pros
  - Up to 5 users, with permission settings (read only, admin, etc.)
  - Easy to generate reports
  - Easy to catch mistakes
  - Bank account linking/monitoring
  - Online invoicing options with credit card payments
  - Online check writing or wire transfers for reimbursements
- Cons
  - Some learning curve
  - Effort to transfer legacy system to quickbooks
  - Annual expense
  - Overkill (many functions are not needed for BSA troops)
