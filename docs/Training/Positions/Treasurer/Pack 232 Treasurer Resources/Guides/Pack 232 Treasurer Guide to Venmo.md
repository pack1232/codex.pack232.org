# Overview
Our Venmo account used to be @pack232money and was setup as a business profile under Josh McWilliam’s account (@jmcwilliam). This was the only way to setup such an account in 2021 and continues to be the only option in November of 2024.

This did become a popular way for parents to pay.

However, several reasons combined to make us decide to stop accepting Venmo payments in 2022 and entirely closing the business profile (after they kept sneaking in) sometime in the 2023/2024 timeframe.
## Why We Don’t Currently Use It
While it was popular, we have decided in 2022 to stop using it for the following reasons:

With the accounting/tax law changes of 2022 that result in all accounts receiving more than $600 receiving tax forms and our current charter organization’s (Selkirk Fire Department) expressed desire to not want to complicate their accounting, we have stopped using it since then.
As of 2022, Venmo doesn’t fully support business accounts like Paypal does so at the moment I’m not sure if we can separate the two of these as it is more of a “profile” than a full fledged “account”. At the time (~Oct 2021) was the only way to set it up as a part of Josh McWilliam’s personal account, which is not a good way to do this as a matter or practice and due to the fact that he would personally be getting the tax documents associated solely with his social security number (rather than our charter organizations EIN).
## Why We Might Reconsider in the Future
It was a popular option and so we should reconsider this, noting the following:

We may be able to get on the same page with our charter organization, however the next item is really the most important…
They may build out the Venmo Business Profiles concept. While it still looks (as of Nov. 2024) to be tied to a personal account, perhaps they will add functionality to further separate things (i.e. not require someone logging into Josh’s personal account or Josh getting Tax documents at the end of the year).
We may be able to simply accept Venmo using Paypal account (probably the better solution as Paypal can be properly associated with our charter organization’s EIN and not Josh’s personal SSN alone).

# Legacy Instructions on Managing Our Venmo Account
I will keep whatever documentation we previously had here for future reference both to understand past transactions and in case we end up setting up a new account in the future.
## Accessing our Venmo Account
Our Venmo account is @pack232money and is setup as a business profile under Josh McWilliam’s account (@jmcwilliam). Accessing it requires signing in Josh’s account and then clicking in the upper left dropdown to change the profile the the business profile.
## Reconciling Venmo Account in Quickbooks

### Accounting for Venmo Fees

## Save Monthly Venmo Statement
Login to Venmo using Josh’s account.
In the left hand side click “Statements”
Select the “Cub Scout Pack 232” profile and the month in question.
Click “Download CSV” under Fees (Sales tax collected report not needed)
Upload it to the pack232money@gmail.com Google Drive account to the Accounts > Vemno > [CurrentYear] folder.
## Save Annual Tax Documents

At the end of January to early February Venmo should provide tax documents (1099-K I think) that we should download and save. These can be accessed when logging into their site under Settings > Tax Documents.

## References
The Fiscal Policies and Procedures for BSA Units (local copy) document published in 2022 says the following about Paypal accounts: “PayPal/Venmo: PayPal can be used to accept unit payments. As of January 1, 2022, the IRS introduced new reporting requirements for payments received for goods and services, lowering the threshold to $600. Third party settlement organizations, such as PayPal and Venmo, will be required to provide customers with a 1099-K form if they receive $600 or more in goods and services transactions during the 2022 tax year. Charter Organization Units and Council Registered Units utilizing PayPal or Venmo should ensure they are using appropriate EINs and following all policies and procedures. Parents of/Groups of Citizens Units should consult their own tax advisors.”
