# Youth Protection Training (YPT)

**Source:** [Scouting America](https://www.scouting.org/training/youth-protection/)

## Overview

Youth Protection Training (YPT) is a mandatory training course required by the Boy Scouts of America (Scouting America) for all registered adult volunteers and employees. The training educates adults on how to identify, prevent, and respond to abuse, and establishes the policies and barriers that protect youth in Scouting programs.

YPT is a **joining requirement** for all registered adults. No adult may register or re-register with the BSA without completing Youth Protection Training. The training must be **renewed every two years**.

---

## Who Must Take YPT

- **All registered adult leaders and volunteers** — required before submitting a new or renewal adult application
- **Parents and guardians** — strongly encouraged, even if not registered as leaders
- **All BSA employees**
- **Merit badge counselors**
- **Any adult accompanying a Scouting unit on an outing or activity**

---

## How to Take YPT

1. **Log in to my.Scouting.org** at [https://my.scouting.org](https://my.scouting.org)
2. Navigate to the **Training Center** (or use the menu to find Youth Protection Training)
3. Select **Youth Protection Training** from the available courses
4. Complete all modules and pass the assessment at the end
5. Print or save your completion certificate for your records

The training is available online and takes approximately **60–90 minutes** to complete.

---

## Key Topics Covered

The YPT course covers the following areas:

- **Types of abuse** — physical, emotional, sexual abuse, and neglect
- **Recognizing signs of abuse** — behavioral and physical indicators
- **BSA Barriers to Abuse** — the policies and guidelines that create a safe environment
  - Two-deep leadership
  - No one-on-one contact between adults and youth
  - Respect for privacy
  - Separate accommodations for youth and adults
  - Appropriate attire and content
  - Constructive discipline
  - Hazing and bullying prevention
  - Cyber safety and digital communication policies
- **Reporting procedures** — mandatory reporting obligations and how to report suspected abuse
- **Response protocols** — how to respond when a youth discloses abuse

---

## BSA Barriers to Abuse

The following barriers are central to BSA Youth Protection policies:

| Barrier | Description |
|---|---|
| **Two-Deep Leadership** | Two registered adult leaders, or one registered adult and a parent, are required at all Scouting activities |
| **No One-on-One Contact** | One-on-one contact between an adult and a youth is prohibited, in person and online |
| **Respect for Privacy** | Adults must respect the privacy of youth in situations such as changing clothes and bathing |
| **Separate Accommodations** | Youth and adults sleep in separate tents/areas; male and female youth have separate accommodations |
| **Appropriate Content** | All materials and conversations must be age-appropriate |
| **Constructive Discipline** | Physical punishment and hazing are prohibited |
| **Digital Communication** | Electronic communications between adults and youth must include another adult or parent |

## Renewal Requirements

- YPT certification **expires every two years** from the date of completion
- Leaders must retake the full training course to renew
- An expired YPT will prevent re-registration as an adult volunteer
- Units should track YPT expiration dates for all registered adults
- Renewal reminders are available through my.Scouting.org

---

## Reporting Abuse

If you suspect or become aware of child abuse:

1. **Ensure the immediate safety of the youth**
2. **Report to local authorities** — call 911 if a youth is in immediate danger
3. **Contact Scouts First Helpline** at **1-844-SCOUTS1** (1-844-726-8871)
4. **Notify your local council** Scout Executive
5. **Document** the incident in writing as soon as possible

All adults in Scouting are **mandatory reporters** and are required by BSA policy to report suspected abuse regardless of state mandatory reporting laws.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Is YPT required before attending my first meeting as a new leader?**
A: Yes. Youth Protection Training must be completed before an adult application can be submitted and approved.

**Q: Can I take YPT in person instead of online?**
A: The primary delivery method is online through my.Scouting.org. Some councils may offer in-person sessions; check with your local council.

**Q: How do I verify my YPT completion?**
A: Log in to my.Scouting.org and check the Training section of your profile. Your completion date and expiration date are listed there.

**Q: Does YPT count toward being "position trained"?**
A: YPT is a prerequisite for all position-trained requirements but alone does not make a leader "position trained." Additional position-specific courses are required.
