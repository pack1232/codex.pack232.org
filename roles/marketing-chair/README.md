# Marketing / Social Media Chair

## Role Summary

The Marketing Chair keeps Pack 232's activities visible — to current pack families, to the broader community, and to prospective Scouts. This includes managing the pack's Facebook page, posting event photos, promoting recruitment events, issuing press releases for major events, and participating in community visibility activities like activities fairs.

---

## Requirements

- **Age**: 21 years or older
- **Registration**: Registered adult leader of BSA; current with YPT
- **Selection**: Selected by the Committee Chair
- **Required Training**:
  - Youth Protection Training (YPT) — renew every 2 years
  - Pack Committee Challenge (online at my.Scouting.org)
- **Access**: Posting access to pack Facebook page; may have website access

---

## Core Responsibilities

- Take photos at pack meetings and outings; post them to the pack Facebook page
- Create Facebook events for pack meetings and outings
- Provide press releases to local press on major pack events (Pinewood Derby, Blue & Gold, recruitment)
- Identify and participate in events to enhance the visibility of the pack (activities fairs, community events)
- Circulate Cub Scout recruiting fliers and brochures
- Coordinate with Membership Chair on recruitment event promotion
- Maintain the inventory of Pack 232 Class B t-shirts
- Keep pack activities visible to the chartered organization (Glenmont Elementary PTA) and community

---

## Social Media (Facebook)

The pack's primary social media presence is its Facebook page.

**Posting responsibilities:**
- Pack meeting events (created in advance with date, time, location)
- Event photos within 1–2 days of the event
- Recruitment announcements (school-night sign-ups, activities fairs)
- Major milestone posts (Blue & Gold, crossover, Pinewood Derby results)
- Community engagement (commenting, responding to questions)

**Photo policy**: Only post photos of Scouts whose families have given consent. If uncertain, omit faces or get explicit permission. Follow BSA's youth protection guidelines for photo sharing.
